The beginner's guide to AI in the newsroom 04 / NN

Part one · Why it matters

Why not a free account?

Free Claude, personal ChatGPT, Gemini on your own Google account: tools built for the public, not for a newsroom.

  • It can train the next model What you paste in can be used to train future models. On most free accounts that is on unless you switch it off.
  • People can read it Companies have staff and contractors review some conversations to improve their products.
  • Deleted isn’t always gone Chats can be kept for months or years. In 2025 a US court ordered OpenAI to keep ChatGPT chats, deleted ones included, as evidence in the New York Times’s lawsuit.
  • It can end up in public In 2025 thousands of shared ChatGPT conversations turned up in Google search results.
  • The law still applies Names and details of real people are personal data. Putting them into a tool with no Guardian agreement can breach data protection law.

Instead

Your Guardian account

Gemini under the Guardian’s agreement with Google. Your content is not used for training outside the Guardian’s domain, and your conversations are not reviewed outside it.

Check the account in the top corner before you paste anything in.

Part one · The rest of the rules

Four more things to know.

  • Rule 01

    No Gemini icon? Do the training

    Gemini appears once you have done the AI Essentials training and read the GenAI acceptable use policy.

  • Rule 02

    If it can’t go in Drive, it can’t go in AI

    A confidential source or a sensitive document stays out of AI tools, for the same reasons it stays out of email.

  • Rule 03

    Take care with people

    Don’t use AI to form opinions about individuals, or to make decisions about them without the data privacy team. Know where your information came from.

  • Rule 04

    New tools need approval

    Want something that isn’t here? You need budget, then Tech Council approval, before it touches Guardian material.

Part two · How far to trust it

How reliable are AI Overviews?

Right on hard factual questions

91%

An independent test of 4,326 questions for the New York Times. Google disputes the test set.

Of those right answers, not fully backed by their sources

56%

The answer was right, but the pages it linked to did not fully show it.

So use it to get your bearings.

  • Fine for orientation A term, a date, a name: things you would check anyway.
  • Not a source Click through to the page it cites before you use anything.
  • Weakest where it matters A Guardian investigation in January 2026 found health overviews giving advice the opposite of expert guidance.

Oumi for the New York Times, October 2025 and February 2026. A second study, from Washington University in St Louis in May 2026 and not yet peer reviewed, found 89% of 98,000 claims matched the pages cited. The Guardian, 2 January 2026.

Part two · 02 · AI Mode

Ask the whole question.

AI Mode is a separate tab on Google that answers in a conversation instead of a list of links. Find it on the results page, or go to google.com/aimode. It can read a PDF or a photo you upload, too.

Try this in AI Mode

Which English councils have issued section 114 notices since 2018, and what reasons did each give? Put it in a table with a link to the source for each one.

  1. "since 2018, and what reasons did each give?"

    Ask every part in one go. AI Mode runs many searches at once, so a question you would have split into five searches becomes one.

  2. "with a link to the source for each one"

    Ask for the sources and you can check every line. Then follow up in the same box. It remembers the conversation.

Part two · 03 · Gemini in Chrome

Ask the page you’re on.

Gemini in Chrome sits in your browser. It can read the page in front of you, or several open tabs at once, so you don’t have to copy anything out.

01

Open the page

In Chrome, signed in with your Guardian account. If the site needs a login, log in first.

02

Click the Gemini icon

At the top of Chrome. Or right-click anywhere on the page and choose Ask Gemini.

03

Ask, then check

Ask about this page, or name your other tabs. Check the answer against the page itself.

In practice

Three gov.uk pages open? Ask it to compare the eligibility rules on all three. One answer instead of three careful reads.

Part two · 04 · Gemini in YouTube

Ask a video a question.

01

What does it cover?

Click Ask under the video and ask for a summary with timestamps. You know in a minute whether a two-hour hearing is worth watching.

02

Where is it said?

Ask when the minister talks about prisons. Click the timestamp and listen to the exact words before you quote them.

03

No Ask button?

It shows on some videos and not others. Paste the link into the Gemini app and ask there instead.
