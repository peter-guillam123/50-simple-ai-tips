# Practical AI ideas for Guardian journalists: a research list

27 October 2026. Prepared for Chris Moran as the basis for rebuilding the tips. Nothing here is built yet.

## What this is

A long list of concrete things a reporter, producer or editor can do with the tools the Guardian provides. Every idea fits the guide's rules: nothing writes copy for publication, nothing needs confidential material in an outside tool, and each names one best tool.

The ideas are grouped by the job a journalist is trying to do, because that is how you want the new front end to work. Each idea has a title that says plainly what it does, a line on when you would use it, the tool, and two tags.

**Status** says how it relates to the current page:

- **Keep**: an existing tip, title fine.
- **Retitle**: an existing tip with a clearer title (old title in brackets).
- **Merge**: fold into another idea.
- **New**: not on the page yet.

**Basis** says how solid the idea is:

- **Seen**: documented newsroom practice or survey evidence (sources at the end).
- **Trainers**: recommended by journalism trainers, such as Paul Bradshaw at the Online Journalism Blog or the EBU's verification team.
- **Proposed**: my own suggestion. Plausible, untested on a Guardian account.

★ marks the ideas I would lead with: the clearest, most journalistic, most likely to convert a sceptic.

## What the research found

- **What UK journalists actually use AI for.** The Reuters Institute surveyed 1,004 UK journalists (August to November 2024). The share using AI for each task at least monthly: transcription 49%, translation 33%, grammar checking and copy-editing 30%, story research 22%, idea generation 16%, fact-checking and verification 12%. The everyday uses are language chores, not writing. The guide should lead with those and with research.
- **Documents are where AI earns its keep for reporters.** The best-documented uses are reading agendas, reports, court records and document dumps, with page references kept so the reporter can check. Bay City News uses Gemini to read meeting agendas and keeps page and paragraph references in every summary. An Investigate Midwest reporter used NotebookLM to question the documents behind a story.
- **Meeting monitoring is the strongest local-news use.** Connecticut Public, Hearst and others watch council meeting videos, transcribe them and send summaries to reporters. They describe it as a way to find stories, not to write them. A reporter can do a manual version of this with YouTube and Gemini.
- **Quotes are the main danger.** A test that loaded old Times archives into NotebookLM found it produced quotes that did not exist, even when asked about its sourcing. Every idea that touches quotes below says: check against the recording or the page.
- **The Guardian's own direction.** The March 2026 update to the editorial code requires training and senior-editor approval for substantial use. Reports say the Guardian is building in-house tools for image descriptions, archive search, document analysis and transcription. That fits leading with Ask the Guardian, House and Guardian Angles where they do the job.
- **Gaps.** I found little documented practice for specific desks (sport, business, audio, pictures). Most desk-specific ideas below are marked Proposed and need a reporter from that desk to test them.

### B. Find a story

6. ★ **Turn a long report into a list of possible stories.** New. Notebook. Trainers.
   The day an NAO, Ofsted or inquiry report lands. Ask what would surprise a general reader, with the page for each point, then read those pages yourself.
7. ★ **Pick out the agenda items worth covering at a council meeting.** New. Notebook. Seen.
   Load the agenda and reports pack. Ask which items involve money, closures or a change of policy, with page references.
8. **Get a summary of a council meeting video, with timestamps.** New. Gemini app. Seen.
   Paste the link to a meeting you could not attend. Ask what was decided and when each decision was taken, then watch those moments.
9. **Rank a pile of press releases or research abstracts by news value.** New. Gemini app. Trainers.
   Paste twenty abstracts or releases. Tell it who reads you, and ask it to rank them and say why.
10. **Find angles you have not thought of.** New. Gemini app. Trainers.
    Describe the story and who reads it. Ask for angles a local, business or health reporter would take. It does not know your readers unless you say.
11. ★ **Spot what changed between this year's report and last year's** (What changed between versions?). Retitle. Notebook. Proposed.
    Annual reports, manifestos, bills at each stage. Ask what is new, what has gone and what has been reworded.
12. **Get a morning digest of your patch** (A morning news digest). Retitle. Gemini app. Proposed.
13. **See which rows in a dataset stand out.** Merge into 38.

### C. Research and background

14. ★ **Learn a new beat's jargon in ten minutes** (Explain it like I'm new). Retitle. Gemini app. Proposed.
15. ★ **Commission a cited background briefing while you do something else** (A backgrounder in ten minutes). Retitle. Gemini app, Deep Research. Trainers.
16. ★ **Find what an MP or minister has said in Parliament about a subject.** New. House. Proposed.
    Before an interview or when a policy shifts. Needs House's real name, link and steps from you.
17. ★ **Check what the Guardian has already published on a subject.** New. Ask the Guardian. Proposed.
    Before you pitch or file. Needs the tool's link and steps.
18. **See how the Guardian's coverage of an issue has changed over the years.** New. Guardian Angles. Proposed.
19. **Build a dated timeline of a long-running story from cuttings** (A timeline from a pile of cuttings). Retitle. Notebook. Proposed.
20. **Understand a court judgment: who won, on what grounds, and the key paragraphs.** New. Notebook. Proposed.
    Load the judgment. Ask for the ruling, the reasons and the paragraph numbers, then read those paragraphs.
21. **Explain a scientific or medical paper in plain English, with its limits.** New. Notebook. Trainers.
    Ask what the study found, how many people it covered, and what the authors say it cannot show. Then ring an expert.
22. **Profile a company or charity from its own filings.** New. Notebook. Proposed.
    Load Companies House accounts or a Charity Commission report. Ask who the directors are, what changed year on year, and where.
23. **Put every party's position on an issue in one table.** New. Notebook. Proposed.
    Load all the manifestos, not some. Ask for a table of each party's commitment, with page references.
24. **Read a source in another language** (Read a source in another language). Keep. Gemini app. Seen.
25. **Trace a statistic back to where it started** (Where did that number come from?). Retitle. Search, AI Mode. Proposed.

### D. Get through documents

26. ★ **Get answers from a long report, with the page for each one** (Ask a 300-page report anything). Retitle. Notebook. Seen.
27. ★ **Check any answer against the passage it came from** (Every answer comes with a receipt). Retitle. Notebook. Seen.
28. ★ **Pull every date, figure or commitment out of a document into a table** (Turn a document into a data table). Retitle. Notebook. Proposed.
29. **Find every mention of a person or company across a pile of documents.** New. Notebook. Seen.
    A document dump or a set of disclosures. Ask for every mention, with the document and page.
30. **Check whether two statements contradict each other.** New. Notebook. Seen.
    Load the press conference transcript and the written statement. Ask where the wording differs.
31. **Read an FOI response quickly: what was released and what was withheld.** New. Notebook. Proposed.
    Ask which exemptions were used and for which parts.
32. **Summarise a PDF without leaving Drive** (Summarise a PDF where it sits). Merge into 26.
33. **Get a map of a long report before you read it** (Map a sprawling report). Keep, lower down. Notebook. Proposed.
34. **Listen to a summary of a report on the way in** (Listen to it on the way in). Keep. Notebook. Proposed.

### E. Work with numbers

38. **Ask a spreadsheet which rows stand out, and chart them** (Ask your spreadsheet a question). Retitle. Sheets. Proposed.

42. **Turn a wide table into a long one, and check nothing was lost.** New, lower down. Gemini app. Trainers.

### F. Interviews and recordings

43. ★ **Transcribe an interview** (Transcribe a recording). Retitle. Guardian transcription tool. Seen.
44. **Get a live transcript of a press conference as it happens.** Keep. Live transcription extension. Seen.
45. ★ **Find the moment something is said in a two-hour hearing** (Jump to the moment). Retitle. YouTube. Proposed.
46. **Get a summary of a hearing or press conference from its link** (Ask a video to summarise itself; No Ask button? Paste the link). Merge the two. YouTube or Gemini app. Seen.
47. ★ **Prepare questions for an interview from everything they have said before** (The interviewee notebook). Retitle. Notebook. Proposed.
48. **Rehearse a tough interview with a press officer who will not answer.** New. Gemini Live. Trainers.
    Ask it to play an evasive press officer for a named organisation, and practise your follow-ups. Do not ask it to play a real person.
49. **Pull every quote from your transcript, with timestamps.** New. Notebook. Proposed.
    From a transcript made with the Guardian tool. Check each quote against the recording: the research found invented quotes.
50. **Test yourself on the facts before an interview** (Quiz yourself before the interview). Keep, lower down. Notebook. Proposed.

### G. Check and verify

51. ★ **Search with a photo to see where else it has appeared.** New. Google Lens. Seen.
    Reverse image search is the method verification teams use most. The camera icon in the Google search box does it.
52. **Get leads on where a photo was taken, then confirm them on a map.** New. Gemini app. Trainers.
    Ask it to list what in the picture could place it, step by step. Treat its answer as leads only.
53. ★ **Check a quote against the recording.** New. Notebook. Seen.
    Add the video or transcript. Ask where the words appear and check the timestamp yourself.
54. ★ **Check every figure in your copy against the source document.** New. Notebook. Proposed.
    Load the report, paste your paragraph, ask whether the report supports each figure and on which page.
55. ★ **Ask what a critic, the subject or a lawyer would challenge in your story.** New. Gemini app. Trainers.
    Paste your draft. This finds weak spots; it does not replace the legal read.
56. **Find the claims in your draft that rest on one source.** Merge into 55.

## Current tips I would drop or demote

- **Drop as separate tips:** "Summarise a PDF where it sits" (merge into 26), the two YouTube summary tips (merge into 46), "Ask your spreadsheet a question" as a separate idea from 38.
- **Demote to the end of their group:** "Map a sprawling report", "Quiz yourself", the two Meet tips, "Catch up on a busy Chat space". They are useful but not journalism.
- **Nothing dropped outright.** Even the generic ones help a sceptic, but they should not lead.

## How the titles change

The old titles often worked as teasers. The new ones say what you do and what you get, starting with a verb:

| Old | New |
| --- | --- |
| A second pair of eyes | Proofread your copy |
| Jump to the moment | Find the moment something is said in a two-hour hearing |
| Every answer comes with a receipt | Check any answer against the passage it came from |
| The interviewee notebook | Prepare questions for an interview from everything they have said before |
| What am I missing? | List the questions your story has not answered yet |
| Explain it like I'm new | Learn a new beat's jargon in ten minutes |

## For the new front end

You asked to drop the "50" and lead with jobs. Groups B to J above would make the job filter: **Find a story, Research a subject, Get through documents, Work with numbers, Interviews and recordings, Check and verify, Organise your work, Before you file, Make it routine.** That replaces the current nine job ids, which the project notes say only you can change. "Start with what you have" could stay as a second way in.
